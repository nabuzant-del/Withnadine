# Nadine's private notes

For Nadine only. These go in the chat as text. They never go into the PDF, the email, or any content.
Write them in English, with Arabic phrases wherever she used Arabic. The tone is a peer reviewing a session honestly and kindly: specific, not flattering.

## 1. Template
```
⚑ CARE FLAG: <one line, only if there is one>

SESSION IN 5 LINES
1. Who came in and with what (first name, the time zone she lives in)
2. The core tension
3. The turn: the moment something shifted
4. What she's leaving with
5. What's open

SKILLS
Covered well: <skill numbers + one line on why each landed>
Rushed or skipped: <skill numbers + where it would have fitted>

GAPS: CLEARER WORDING
• [mm:ss] What you said: «…» → Try: «…» (why: one line)
(2–4 of these, from real moments where she looked lost or went back to the old story)

QUESTIONS YOU COULD HAVE ASKED
• «…» at [mm:ss], after she said «…»
(2–3)

FOLLOW UP NEXT SESSION
• What to ask about first
• Anything you promised (in the session or by email)
• Her booking status (from Gmail / Stan, if known)

FROM HER EMAILS
• What she hoped for · language she writes in · anything pending
(or: "Gmail not checked")
```

## 2. Care flag protocol
A care flag is raised when the client mentions any of these:
- self-harm or suicide
- harm to or from someone
- abuse
- a medical crisis
- a child at risk
- anything that looks like it needs a clinician rather than a coach

When there is a flag:
- Write one neutral line at the very top of the notes: what was mentioned and the timestamp. Don't interpret it, diagnose it, or add detail.
- Add: "Consider following up personally, and share a professional or crisis resource from your referral list if it fits."
- **Keep it out of the PDF and the email entirely.** Pass the relevant words to `--private` so the build blocks if any of them slip in.
- If it involves risk to her or to someone else, **don't make the email draft**. Tell Nadine to reach her personally first.
- The session is never used for content. The content skill will refuse it.

Don't list the six-skill gaps on a care-flagged session unless Nadine asks. Her attention belongs on the client first.
